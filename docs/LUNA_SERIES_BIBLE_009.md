# Luna series rules and cast review — 2026-10-26

These owner instructions govern future cartoon work and supersede the narrated format of the first three-minute experiment. The earlier prototype remains preserved as an experiment, not the series template.

## Dialogue and comprehension

- The cartoon is dialogue-led. Luna and the animals talk as cartoon characters.
- Mike and Nicol are the only humans who understand Luna. Other humans hear ordinary dog sounds, which creates room for misunderstandings and visual jokes.
- Do not silently extend this to Mike and Nicol understanding every other animal; that broader rule has not been specified.
- Use narration only for a particular scene that actually needs explanation. Do not build the series as a continuously narrated storybook.
- Future opening work needs conversations, reactions and purposeful scene acting. The first narrated prototype does not establish a permanent narrator or Luna's character voice.

## Permanent Luna voice — approval gate

The owner said the voice used for Luna, once liked/approved, becomes her permanent voice. No specific new Luna character voice is approved or locked yet. `af_heart` in the first prototype was a separate narrator, not an approved replacement for Luna's established voice. Do not promote it into Luna's permanent voice by assumption.

When the owner approves a character-voice sample, preserve its WAV/reference clip and record: voice engine and version; model/weights identifier and SHA256; exact preset/voice ID and any blend; style, speed, pitch and processing settings; sample rate; name/location pronunciation choices; license/source; approval date; reference file identity and SHA256. Keep the reference and settings together. Reuse them consistently; a narrator or temporary test voice is a separate approval.

## All-ages family standard — owner instruction, 2026-10-26
The series is for all ages and targets G-rated family entertainment. Apply this to scripts, dialogue, designs, character acting, voices, music, sound effects and promotional material. This is a creative target, not a claim of an official classification.

Keep humor warm, clever and accessible to children and adults. Exclude profanity, sexual material, graphic injury, cruelty and intense frightening imagery. Use gentle adventure stakes, mild brief suspense, harmless visual comedy and reassuring resolutions. Do not rely on adult innuendo to entertain adults.

The Rook remains a charming bad guy: witty schemes, playful trickery, vanity and comic setbacks, rather than brutality, disturbing threats or horror. His silhouette and expressions may be theatrical, but scenes should keep him enjoyable rather than terrifying. Let Luna counter him through kindness, courage, curiosity and cleverness. Preserve meaningful conflict without harsh punishment or humiliating vulnerable characters.
