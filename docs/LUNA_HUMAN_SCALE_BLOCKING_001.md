# Luna human-scale villain blocking study — draft 001

**Status:** timing and motion proposal only. No story, character design, final animation, dialogue, sound, lighting, or owner approval is implied.

## Purpose

Apply the story-first, timing-first workflow to a short motion test for the human-sized villain. Test readability, proximity, weight transfer and contrast in pace before any visual polish. Preserve the current approved 2D character artwork and proportions; this sheet describes staging and motion only.

## 8-second animatic map

| Time | Beat | Blocking and timing | Review question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:01.75 | Hold | Eye-level two-character composition. The villain occupies ordinary human scale in the shared space. Hold a quiet, readable pose; keep clear negative space between characters. | Can the silhouette and relationship read without camera tricks? |
| 0:01.75–0:02.50 | Attention shifts | Let the eyes lead, then turn the head. Keep the torso and feet planted through the first part of the move. | Does the audience see the intention before the body moves? |
| 0:02.50–0:03.75 | Deliberate advance | One measured step toward the other character. Transfer weight through pelvis, torso and planted foot; avoid sliding. The second character gives a small, readable response only if that reaction is already in the approved story. | Does the move feel controlled and grounded? |
| 0:03.75–0:04.75 | Settle | Let the body finish traveling, then settle the shoulders and hands. Hold long enough to read the new distance. | Is the new proximity clear and uncomfortable without enlarging the villain? |
| 0:04.75–0:05.50 | Pace break | One brief, faster head or hand action after the stillness. Keep it inside the established pose language. | Does the change in pace land, or does it feel like a random twitch? |
| 0:05.50–0:08.00 | Hold / end pose | Return to stillness with a small eye or breath cue, then end on a clean pose that can cut. | Does the held ending carry more tension than adding more movement? |

Frame guide: 24 fps, 192 frames total. Beat boundaries are approximate and should be adjusted in the animatic based on readability, not forced to exact durations.

## Staging rules

- Keep camera and eye lines at character height. Establish ordinary human scale through shared floor plane, consistent perspective and comparable body proportions.
- Use screen position, overlap and distance to communicate threat. Do not use a giant silhouette, dramatic low-angle size distortion, extra limbs, or rapid camera push to make the villain seem larger.
- Keep the first pose long enough to read. Initiate with gaze, follow with head and torso, and complete with the step and weight settle.
- Keep feet anchored during holds and weight changes. Show a clear contact pose before the next step; do not let the character skate.
- Use one controlled action and one deliberate pace change in this test. Do not add continuous idle motion to fill the holds.
- Preserve the supplied 2D model sheet, facial features, proportions and approved palette exactly. Add no new costume, face, anatomy, expression, story beat or voice choice in this motion pass.

## Approval gates

1. **Story/beat approval:** confirm that the action fits the scene’s approved intent.
2. **Animatic approval:** review timing, camera, screen direction and readability using rough poses only.
3. **Blocking approval:** review silhouette, contact, weight transfer and holds before cleanup.
4. **Polish review:** only after the first three gates, refine drawing, acting, lighting and sound.
5. **Final owner approval:** required before calling the shot final or using it as a production reference.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Human scale reads consistently from first frame to last.
- [ ] The initial pose is readable at a glance.
- [ ] Eye direction leads the move.
- [ ] The step has clear foot contact and visible weight transfer.
- [ ] The settling pose holds long enough to read.
- [ ] The faster action contrasts with the calm section and still feels intentional.
- [ ] The 2D artwork remains on-model and unchanged.
- [ ] Rough timing works before drawing cleanup or sound polish.
- [ ] Any story-dependent reaction has already been approved.

## What this does not claim

This is a paper animatic plan, not rendered animation or a completed performance. It does not revise the villain’s design, decide the scene’s story, or approve any movement. The shot’s exact emotional intention and the existing scene context should be checked against the approved storyboard before animation begins.
